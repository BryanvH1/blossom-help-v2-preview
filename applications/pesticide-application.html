    Recording a Pesticide Application

    Record each spray once, and Blossom carries it through to your posting sheet and your Pesticide Use Report — so the care you take here is what makes reporting easy later.

    Opening the right screen

    1. Tap Applications in the sidebar.
    2. Tap New application. Four choices appear:
      • Log pesticide — a spray that has happened (this page)
      • Log nutrient application — see Nutrient Applications
      • New pesticide work order — a spray planned for later
      • New nutrient work order
    3. Choose Log pesticide.

    Logging versus ordering

    Log records something that happened. New … work order writes down a job to be done, which someone completes later — see Work orders. Same products, different moment.

    How the screen is laid out

    Two panels that work together.

    On the left — the application itself:

    • Method — Air, Ground, Sprinklers or Wipe.
    • Target — the pest you sprayed for.
    • Date — required.
    • Completed — whether the job is done.
    • ADVANCED — times, weather and a note.
    • APPLICATIONS — starts empty and fills in as you build the spray.

    On the right — three tabs you work through:

    Tab What you pick
    Beds The beds sprayed, grouped by block with each bed's variety — A1 — Mullica Queen. All takes a whole block; there's also Select all, Filters and a search
    Products Grouped Fungicide · Herbicide · Insecticide · Plant growth regulator · Adjuvant, with a filter. Adjuvants live in this same list
    Applicator Who applied it

    Each tab shows a running count once you've chosen — Beds · 1 bed, Products · 1 product.

    Two fields arrive already filled in

    Method loads set to GROUND, and Completed loads switched on. Both are right for the commonest case — a ground spray you have just finished — and neither announces itself. An aerial application saved without changing Method files as a ground application, which is a wrong compliance record. Check both before you save.

    Building the spray

    1. On the Beds tab, pick every bed that was sprayed.
    2. On the Products tab, pick every product in the tank.

    3. Look at the APPLICATIONS panel on the left. Each product now appears as a card with a unit selector (fl oz/ac) and, under it, one row per bed:

      Abound · fl oz/ac · 1 bed └ A1 · Applicator · rate · fl oz

    4. Enter the rate for each row, and set the applicator if it differs.

    5. Fill in Method, Target and Date on the left, and open ADVANCED for start and end times, weather and notes.
    6. Tap Log pesticide — or Save & log another if more sprays went on the same day. Clear empties the form and starts over.

    Rate is per product, per bed

    This is why rate isn't a single box at the top. Three products across four beds gives you twelve rows, each with its own rate — which is what makes the quantities on your posting sheet and PUR come out right.

    The restrictions box updates as you work

    As soon as there's a product in the mix, RESTRICTIONS FOR THIS MIX appears in the left panel and recalculates with every change:

    Binding hold: REI 4 h — Abound · do not enter without PPE until — No PHI on file for this mix — check each product label before harvesting.

    Watch it while you add products

    Add a 48-hour product to a tank of 4-hour ones and the binding hold jumps to 48 hours in front of you — before you have committed to the mix. That is the moment to reconsider, not after the sprayer is empty. How this is worked out explains the rule; the label always governs.

    After you save

    One accurate record feeds three things automatically:

    • The posting and the application record — see Pesticide posting & restricted-entry (REI) signs.
    • Your Pesticide Use Report — see Submitting your PUR.
    • Your Ocean Spray or Agrian transfer, if you deliver to them — see PUR Transfer.

    Check the beds before you save

    Make sure every bed you actually treated is selected. A missing bed is far harder to spot three months later at reporting time.
