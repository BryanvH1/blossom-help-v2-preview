    Pesticide posting & restricted-entry (REI) signs

    After a spray the field needs a posting sheet showing the restricted-entry interval — how long before anyone may go back in. Blossom builds it from the applications you recorded, so the sheet matches the record.

    The quickest answer: open the application

    You don't need to print anything to find out when a bed is safe to enter. Open any application from the ledger and it shows RESTRICTIONS FOR THIS MIX in plain words:

    Binding hold: REI 12 h — Callisto · do not enter without PPE until Fri, May 29, 2026

    Earliest harvest: Mon, Jul 13, 2026 — longest PHI is 45 d (Callisto).

    Above it, each product in the TANK MIX is listed with its group, manufacturer, rate, the total mixed for the acreage — 18.1 fl oz/ac · 190.96 fl oz for 10.55 ac — and its own REI and PHI. Below it, every bed treated, with its variety and block.

    This is the same calculation as a work order

    A work order shows the identical box before the job; a completed application shows it after. Longest REI governs the whole treated area, longest PHI sets the earliest harvest, and the label always governs over both.

    Two different printouts — don't mix them up

    Blossom prints two documents, for two different purposes. They look similar and are easy to confuse.

    The posting — what goes on the gate

    Open Reports ▸ Pest Management ▸ Pesticide Application Posting, choose the Posting date, and tap Print.

    It carries:

    • The farm, and the date with the time window — September 25, 2026 5:30 am – 8:00 am
    • Restricted Entry — 24 hours, stated near the top
    • A Pesticides table: name, active ingredient, EPA #, rate, REI and PHI
    • The beds it applies to
    • Other Details — applied by (with certification number), method, target pests and weather conditions

    The application record — what goes in the file

    Reached from the ledger, one application at a time. It repeats the same facts but adds the ones an auditor asks for:

    • Total applied per product as well as the rate per acre — 7.82 fl oz/ac · 166.64 fl oz
    • Beds treated with each bed's variety and acres, and a total
    • The work order it came from, if any
    • An Applicator signature and Date signed block
    • A record number in the footer — Record #126161

    One is for the field, one is for the folder

    If someone is about to walk into a bog, they need the posting. If you're answering a question months later about what went on where, you want the application record — it's the one with the signature line.

    Rate is recorded per product, per bed

    An application with three products across four beds holds twelve rates, not one. Blossom multiplies each by the acreage to give the total applied, which is the number that reaches both documents and your PUR — see Recording a Pesticide Application.

    The posted REI is the longest one in the tank

    Where an application mixes products, the sheet posts the longest REI, not each product's own. A tank with a 48-hour product and a 12-hour product posts 48 hours — the figure people re-enter on. The table below still lists each product's individual REI and PHI.

    Record the time window, not just the date

    REI runs from the end of application, and the sheet prints the window you recorded. An application with no times can only be posted from the date, which is coarser than the label requires and usually costs working hours.
